# Love Thy Neighbor: home page brief

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.** The client asked for a
redesign that is "premium, trusted, more editorial rather than a bunch of random
AI generated assets", "not like a world we're flying through", then delegated
the rest: "do what you think is best, use filler numbers".

Evidence is marked **[client]**. Everything else is an authored decision.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** [client] "premium, trusted, more editorial". Authored references:
   a long-read feature in a Sunday magazine; a church bulletin set by a good
   typographer; the documentary plainness of a local newspaper photo essay.
2. **Journey.** Authored: who we are (title page) → the need → one Saturday on
   one porch (the film) → the three programs → the people and partners who make
   it possible → refer a neighbor.
3. **Energy.** Quiet and steady. One rise at the film. A calm, confident end.
4. **Feeling, and the one moment.** Feeling curve below. The moment is the film.
5. **What no other site does.** Authored: the two hands of the LTN mark start
   apart in the margin and close the gap as you read. They clasp, and the heart
   is whole, at the moment you reach "Refer a neighbor".
6. **Range.** [client] Editorial.
7. **One world or distinct scenes.** [client] Not a world to fly through.
   Distinct chapters.
8. **Assets.** [client] Real assets only. They have their own photography
   (1600px maximum, team portraits around 500px), a 13s 720p story film, and
   the logo as a raster PNG. No generation and no spend.

## Step 1 decisions

- **What and who:** a faith-based nonprofit serving seniors in the Lower Rio
  Grande Valley. The visitors are families worried about an elderly relative,
  church members, local businesses, and would-be volunteers.
- **Belief by the end:** Love Thy Neighbor is real, local and careful, and you
  can trust them with someone you love.
- **One action:** **Refer a neighbor.** It is the same label everywhere.
  Volunteer, partner and donate are secondary, set as running text.
- **Art direction:** documentary photography, set like print. Media sits in its
  own column with a caption and never runs under type.

## Journey

```
1  Recognition   a real local organization, named plainly, on paper
2  Concern       the need: homes in disrepair, loneliness, a senior at his door
3  Tenderness    one Saturday, one yard, one woman on her porch (the film)
4  Clarity       three programs, one page each, labelled with fact
5  Trust         the real partners and leaders, named, and the numbers
6  Resolve       refer a neighbor; the hands meet
```

## Feeling curve

| Act | Feeling | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Title page | Calm recognition | Type on paper, a local dateline, one plain promise |
| I. The need | Concern | A senior at his front door; two paragraphs naming what goes wrong |
| Intertitle | Hush (authored silence) | A dark full-stop page with only a chapter title. Intentional: it is the silence before the peak |
| II. A Saturday | **Tenderness (the peak)** | The story film scrubs under the hand: rakes, a knock, her on the porch |
| III. The work | Clarity | Three programs set as an index, with a fact, a photo and a link each |
| IV. The neighbors | Trust | The partners' names in type, the leaders counted, real figures ticking up |
| Colophon | Resolve | Red ground, one question, "Refer a neighbor"; the two hands clasp |

**The peak:** "you scroll and they're raking her yard, and then she comes out
and just sits on her porch watching them work." It is in act II. It has the
biggest span on the page (3.2 viewport-heights), the only video, and the dark,
empty intertitle in front of it.

**It's the site where** two hands slowly reach toward each other while you read,
and close into a heart right as you're asked to refer someone you love.

**Authored silence:** the intertitle before the film. It holds a chapter number
and title on a dark ground and nothing else.

## Numbers

The client said "use filler numbers". Real figures used: **3** programs, **8**
partners and **9** leadership members (all counted from the current site). Two
are filler and **must be replaced before launch**: seniors visited and volunteer
hours. Each one is marked `TK` (the print convention for "to come") while
`<html data-draft>` is set. Remove the attribute once real figures are in.

## Revision 2 (client feedback)

[client] "not a fan" of the porch line; not big on "A Saturday in Harlingen"
("we're only serving Harlingen right now but want to expand as we grow"); "no
need to mark chapters"; "get some stats to back up the need at the front ...
find the most heartbreaking stats"; not big on "Nobody on this street gets
forgotten"; "lean more into what is Love Thy Neighbor"; "don't lock in on one
program for the home page, we want to do a lot".

Changes:

- **Opening:** "Love thy neighbor. Then show up." The text below it names all
  three programs.
- **Chapter numbers and labels removed.** The folio lists section titles only.
- **Evidence section first.** Five figures plus one growth projection, each
  footnoted to its primary source:
  1. 23% of Cameron County residents 65+ live below the poverty line (U.S.
     10.8%). ACS 2020-2024, S1701.
  2. 14,511 Cameron County households are one person 65+ living alone. ACS
     2020-2024, B11007.
  3. Lacking social connection can raise early-death risk as much as smoking
     up to 15 cigarettes a day. U.S. Surgeon General advisory (2023), p. 8.
     This one is a national figure.
  4. 1 in 4 (26.5%) Cameron County residents 65+ have serious difficulty
     walking or climbing stairs. ACS 2020-2024, S1810.
  5. 11,030 Cameron County residents 65+ have difficulty running errands
     alone. ACS 2020-2024, S1810.
  6. Growth: the Valley's 65+ population is projected to go from 175,849 (2020)
     to 271,976 (2040). Texas Demographic Center, Vintage 2024, mid scenario,
     four counties combined.

  Process: five researchers produced 30 candidates. A fact-checker confirmed
  every one against the primary source, and a skeptic re-checked the five that
  were chosen. All of them held.
- **Film:** retitled "Someone at the door". The captions now speak for all of
  LTN's work, the credit names no single program or city, and the peak line is
  "Everyone deserves a neighbor who comes back."
- **Programs section:** "Body, heart and spirit." It says plainly that LTN
  serves Harlingen and Cameron County today and plans to grow across the
  Valley.

**Revised peak sentence:** "they're raking her yard, she comes to the door,
someone sits with her, and then it just says everyone deserves a neighbor who
comes back."
